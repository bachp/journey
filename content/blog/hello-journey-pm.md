Today, journey.pm becomes the public home of The Deliberation Journey — a software-development methodology for the agentic era. The site is plain by design: no tracking, no external requests, no JavaScript — prose.

**What it is.** The pitch in thirty seconds:

> Trunk-based development won for humans, but its trunk only holds code. Everything else a product is — the intent, the decisions, the behavior we promised — lived in developers' heads. Agents don't have heads: whatever they must know has to be materialized.
>
> So we lift trunk-based development by one move. The trunk becomes the baseline, the whole product description under version control. The branch becomes a journey that proposes deltas to any of it, plus the decisions and evidence behind them, passing consensus gates where roles sign off. On return, the deltas merge into the baseline and the journey freezes into an immutable record — the product's memory of why it is the way it is. Code becomes the disposable projection; consensus binds to intent and observable behavior.

The full model is on [The Model](/methodology/); the conformance test is on [Invariants](/invariants/).

**Why a site.** The methodology developed in conversation and in private notes. A public home gives it a stable surface: a URL to point a reader at, a feed to follow, and an `llms.txt` for the agents it's about.

**What's coming.** Comparison deep-dives — OpenSpec, Spec Kit, Kiro, and the other corners of the landscape — and the metrics model: what a deliberation-driven team measures (human touches per gate, gate stall points, record retrieval, trivial-lane share) and what each signal means. The [comparisons page](/comparisons/) carries the condensed map in the meantime.

Code is cheap now, and consensus is the scarce resource. This site is where that claim gets argued in public.
